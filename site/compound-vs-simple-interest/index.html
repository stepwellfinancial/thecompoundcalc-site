Compound vs simple interest, worked through

Simple interest is paid only on the money you put in. Compound interest is also paid on interest you have already earned, so each period's interest is worked out on a bigger balance.

Take $10,000 to start, $100 added at the end of each month, an example rate of 5% and 10 years. With compound interest, compounded monthly, the calculator gives a final balance of $31,998.32. With simple interest it gives $29,975.00. Compounding adds $2,023.32, which is 6.8% more than the simple balance. You put in $22,000.00, and the compound balance also holds $9,998.32 of interest. With the same inputs ($10,000 to start, $100 added at the end of each month, an example rate of 5% compounded monthly), the gap starts small and grows. After year 1 the compound balance is only $12.00 ahead ($11,739.50 against $11,727.50). After year 10 it is $2,023.32 ahead. That is the snowball effect: the longer the money stays in, the more of each year's interest is interest on earlier interest. For illustration only. Real returns vary and are not guaranteed.
